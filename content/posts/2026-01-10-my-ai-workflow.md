How am I adapting to AI at work?

I woke up to the potential of AI in early 2025, when coding agents were arriving on the scene. Things moved past, my org was quick to purchase licenses, and by 2025 end everybody was being asked - how are you using AI?

At work, I started playing with Cursor to breakdown a monolith. Forking a new repository out of an old one, and replicating features one by one. I was blown away by how quick it was at generating code. I soon realised that my comprehension of the code was lagging behind the speed at which it was getting generated and there was no way to verify that things would work. In between I changed jobs. I entered a tough market after four and a half years. I noticed a few companies had allowed their first round to be AI assisted. You could use a coding assistant while solving a problem. This is when I realised, agents had gone mainstream.

In my new job Cursor is my default editor, I rarely use any other IDEs. The workflow I have adapted is still chat based. At present, my workflow has two chat windows, the rubber duck and the SDE. The rubber duck helps me ask questions, fine tune my ideas around how the code works. I use it to create coding prompts, which I feed into the SDE. 

Another popular workflow, is terminal based, I am still finding my way around it. I used it to create this Hugo based blog. HTML/CSS have been my nemesis since start. Codex on the terminal inside Cursor has turned it into a cool breeze.

I use ChatGPT and Gemini for research purposes. I find transliteration to be its best feature. I speak into it, on my phone, while I brew a pour over or while walking to the grocery store. It takes my stream of consciousness, converts it into a coherent question, asks for some clarification and shoots off. By the time I am done with my coffee or my walk, I come back to the result. 

I know there are super users out there, who are making Claude work overnight. I haven’t reached that level yet. 

AI adoption is only going to get more intense this year. Coding agents will become mainstream. Developers will be expected to generate code faster. The tooling is evolving at breakneck speed. Agents have gotten better at writing code, which has opened the stack for developers. The "stack" you identify with feels less important, what counts is that you have a taste for good design. It’s beneficial to have seen through crappy code and broken processes, to understand what “good” looks like. 

I feel beginners can achieve the same by burning fingers with vibe coding.

Time for some prediction, I predict, this year there we will be a wave of custom coding IDEs which will offer templates out of the box, like a Spring Boot skeleton or a Spark ETL wireframes. At the same time, these frameworks might also get upended as the stack gets rewritten. New programming languages might evolve, claiming to be fit for agents not humans. The tooling that gets created will solve for faster shipping and tighter verification. Agents in the dev workflow are well established, soon enough there will be agents running in production tracking logs to send alerts or taking actions like an SRE.

I am optimistic about the software ecosystem. I still don’t see a world where software engineers are done with. However it pans out, it is undeniable that it will be entertaining.
